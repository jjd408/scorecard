Scorecard Help

Scorecard keeps a baseball scorecard at-bat by at-bat. It draws each at-bat like a box on a paper card, keeps the score, and works out batting and pitching stats as you go.

Every window has a Help button that opens this manual at the part about that window. F1 does the same.

Getting started

  • Start it: double-click Scorecard on the desktop, or pick it from the Applications menu (Accessories).
  • Your data: everything is saved as you go, in ~/.scorecard/scorecard.db. There is no Save button. Back up that file to keep a copy of your games.
  • First run: add teams and players, then create a game (see Setting up and Games). After that, the app reopens the last game you had open each time it starts.
  • After changing the code: run mvn package in ~/projects/scorecard, so the desktop button picks up the new version.

The main window

The window has four parts: the buttons and scoreline along the top, a grid of at-bats for each team, and the stats underneath.

  • Buttons (top left): Teams, Players, Games and Abbrevs open the lists you set up the app with. Help opens this manual.
  • Scoreline (top right): each team's runs (R), hits (H) and errors (E), with the away team first.
  • Team tabs: one grid for the home team and one for the away team. The title bar names the open game.
  • The grid: 9 rows, one per batting-order spot, and a column per time through the order. Each cell is one at-bat. A new column appears whenever every column has an at-bat in it.
  • Stats (bottom): batting and pitching tables for the team on the selected tab. The stats tabs and the grid tabs switch together.
  • Divider: drag the bar between the grid and the stats to give either more room.

Setting up

Set up teams, players and scoring abbreviations once; every game uses them. Each list has New, Delete, Edit and Close buttons, and double-clicking a row edits it.

Teams

  • Add MLB Teams adds all 30 Major League teams in one step, skipping any already there.
  • New asks for a name and city.
  • Edit asks whether you are correcting a mistake or recording a real change, such as a move to a new city. Correct a mistake changes the team on every date. Change effective keeps the old name and city for games before that date, so old games still show the team as it was.

Players

  • New asks for the player's name, uniform number, team, and the date they joined.
  • Edit changes a player's team or number from a date you choose, such as a trade. Earlier games keep the old team and number.
  • Delete removes a player as of a date. They still appear in games played before it; nothing is erased.
  • You can also add a player while scoring: the Add button in the batter and pitcher picker makes a new player for that team.

Abbrevs

Abbreviations are the codes you score plays with, such as 1B, K or E.

  • Add Standard Abbrevs adds the usual set: hits (1B, 2B, 3B, HR), reaching base (BB, IBB, HBP, E, FC, CI), outs (K, KL, G, F, L, P, U, FO, IF, SAC, SF, DP, TP) and baserunning (SB, CS, PO, WP, PB, BK, DI).
  • New adds your own, with a description.
  • The stats rely on the standard codes: hits are 1B, 2B, 3B and HR; walks BB and IBB; strikeouts K and KL; stolen bases SB; errors E. A play scored with another code still counts as a plate appearance, but not as a hit, walk or strikeout.

Games

Click Games to list every game, newest first, with its date, game number, away team and home team.

  • New asks for the date, the away team, the home team and the game number. Type dates as YYYY-MM-DD, for example 2026-07-04. Use game number 2 for the second game of a doubleheader. A new game opens straight away.
  • Open opens the selected game in the main window. The title bar then shows it, for example "Scorecard – Oakland Athletics at San Francisco Giants, 2024-08-17".
  • Edit changes a game's details. Once a game has at-bats, only its date and game number can change.
  • Delete removes a game and everything scored in it.

The team lists show each team's name and city as of the game's date. The app remembers the open game and reopens it the next time it starts.

Scoring at-bats

Every label in a cell is a button: left-click to pick or add one, right-click to take one away. The pointer turns into a hand over anything you can click.

Starting an at-bat

An empty cell is gray and lists only its row, column and "Active no". Click Active to start an at-bat there. The cell turns black and lists the batter, pitcher and count.

A new at-bat fills itself in:

  • Inning and order start as the cell's column and row. Change them when a team bats around, so the extra at-bats in the next column still count for the right inning.
  • Batter is the last batter in the same batting-order spot. Pick the lineup once in the first inning and it carries through the game. A substitute you pick carries forward in that spot.
  • Pitcher is the pitcher from the team's previous at-bat. After a pitching change, pick the new pitcher once and the next at-bats follow.

Autofill only fills a blank batter or pitcher; it never replaces someone you picked. In the first inning there is nothing to copy, so pick them yourself.

Picking the batter and pitcher

Clicking Batter or Pitcher opens a list of the team's players as of the game date. Double-click a name, or select it and click OK. Add makes a new player for the team, even when it has none yet. Cancel leaves the at-bat as it was.

The selected at-bat

The last at-bat you clicked is highlighted with a pale yellow background and a blue border, so you can keep your place. It stays highlighted when dialogs open and close, until you click another cell or open a different game.

Recording plays

Click the diamond of an active at-bat to record what happened. The first play is the batter's own result; any later plays move the batter around the bases as a runner.

  • No plays yet: the New Play form opens straight away, with the pitches, balls and strikes copied from the at-bat.
  • Plays already entered: the Plays list opens instead. New adds the next play, starting at the base after the furthest one reached. Edit (or double-click) changes one; Delete removes one.

Example: Batter 1 singles to center, then scores on Batter 3's double. Enter two plays on Batter 1's at-bat: Play 1B, Played to 8, Base First; then Base Home, By batter 3.

How plays are drawn

  • Safe: each base path run is a green line, and each base reached is filled green. Reaching home fills home plate.
  • Out: a red line runs halfway toward the base, with the out's number circled in red beside it. Plays after an out are not drawn.
  • Labels: each play is written inside the diamond beside its base path, as the abbreviation and fielder number with a space between, such as "1B 8" or "E 6", then the By batter spot. A play that reaches no base, like a strikeout, is written in the middle.

The plays tooltip

Rest the mouse on a diamond to see its plays listed in a small table: number, play with description, base, out, fielder, count, By batter and comment. Columns with nothing in them are left out. The tooltip stays until you move the mouse off the diamond. An at-bat with no plays says "No plays yet. Click to add one."

Stats and the scoreline

The stats and scoreline update as you score, counting only active at-bats. Each team's tab shows its batters and the pitchers who faced the other team, each with a Total row. Rest the mouse on a column heading to see what it means.

The scoreline

The top-right corner shows R, H and E for each team, away first, as in a box score. Runs and hits match the batting Totals. Errors count every play scored E against the team in the field, both reaching base on an error and moving up on one later.

Scoring a half-inning, step by step

  1. Click the team's tab (the team at bat).
  2. Click Active in the next cell down the column. The batter and pitcher fill in.
  3. Click Balls and Strikes as pitches are thrown.
  4. When the at-bat ends, click the diamond and enter the batter's result.
  5. When a runner moves up later, click their diamond again and add a play with the new base and By batter.
  6. Set RBIs on the at-bat of the batter who drove in runs.

Troubleshooting

  • Nothing happens when I click a cell: no game is open. Click Games, then open or create one.
  • The batter didn't fill in: nobody has batted in that spot earlier in the game, or the spot's earlier at-bat has no batter picked. Pick the batter once.
  • A player is missing from the picker: the picker shows players on the team as of the game date. Check their team and the date they joined under Players, or use Add.
  • A stat looks wrong: check the first play's abbreviation. Stats only recognize the standard codes listed under Abbrevs, and turned-off at-bats are not counted.
  • The desktop button runs an old version: run mvn package in ~/projects/scorecard.
  • Moving to another computer: copy ~/.scorecard/scorecard.db; it holds all teams, players and games.
